The "Pepto-Bismol Pink" La Femme car refers to the 1955-1956 Dodge La Femme, a car marketed specifically to women, which was known for its pastel pink color scheme and feminine design elements. 
Here's a more detailed look:
Target Audience:
The La Femme was an attempt by Dodge to capture the female market, recognizing that women were increasingly involved in car-buying decisions. 
Color Scheme:
The La Femme was known for its "Dusty Rose" and "Pigeon Grey" color scheme, designed to convey femininity. 
Marketing:
The La Femme was marketed with slogans like "By Special Appointment to Her Majesty... the American Woman". 
Sales:
Despite the marketing efforts, sales of the La Femme were slow, with fewer than 1000 cars sold in the initial year. 
Design:
The La Femme was based on the Chrysler Newport hardtop body and had a clear plastic roof over the passenger compartment.

Orange blue for color blind

Why Orange and Blue Work Well:
Easy Differentiation:
For the most common types of color blindness (red-green deficiencies), blue is perceived as blue, and orange is perceived as a distinct color, making them easy to differentiate.
Complementary Colors:
Orange and blue are complementary colors on the color wheel, which means they create a strong contrast and visual separation.
Avoidance of Problematic Combinations:
Red and green, blue and green, and yellow and violet are often difficult to distinguish for people with color blindness, so avoiding these combinations is a good practice
